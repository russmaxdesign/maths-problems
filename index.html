Division, including halves and quarters

Level one always divides exactly. Level two divides by 2 or by 4 only, so the answer is never worse than a half or a quarter.

Halving an odd number always gives you a .5. If you halve an odd number and get a whole number back, something has been lost.

Order of operations

    Working

    BODMAS

    Brackets, division, multiplication, addition and subtraction. Powers and roots are held back for the next test, so a wrong answer here is about order rather than notation.

    This test works the same way if you were taught BIDMAS, BEDMAS or PEMDAS. They are four names for one rule, and the differences are set out at the bottom of this page.

    Order of priority:

    • Brackets: ( )
    • Orders: powers (e.g. 3²) and roots (e.g. √9)
    • Division and Multiplication: working left to right
    • Addition and Subtraction: working left to right

        BODMAS below zero

        Same rule, same order of priority. The difference is that a subtraction can now take you below zero and the working carries on from there. Every number is still whole.

        Two things to watch. A subtraction still happens in its left to right place, not last. And once a number is negative, adding to it moves it back towards zero rather than away from it.

            BODMAS, BIDMAS, BEDMAS and PEMDAS

            These are four names for the same rule. Orders, Indices and Exponents all mean powers and roots. Parentheses is another word for brackets. Which one you were taught mostly depends on where you went to school: BODMAS and BIDMAS in the UK, Australia and India, BEDMAS in Canada and New Zealand, PEMDAS in the United States.

            The letter order in the third column is misleading in every version. Division and Multiplication sit on the same level and are worked left to right, whichever letter comes first in the name. Addition and Subtraction work the same way. PEMDAS putting M before D does not mean multiplication is done first.
